## Core Loop

OpenHistoria is turn-based in spirit, but time advances in flexible jumps. The player does not move units one tile at a time. Instead, the player issues strategic directives and watches the world respond.

## Directives

Directives are short orders that guide the next simulation jump. They can express policy, diplomacy, military preparation, infrastructure, intelligence, propaganda, or emergency response.

Strong directives include:

- A clear actor or institution.
- A place, population, or foreign counterpart.
- A time horizon.
- A tradeoff or constraint.

## Time Jumps

Time can advance by fixed periods or to the next major development. Fixed jumps are useful when you are executing a plan. The next-event jump is useful when you want the simulation to find the next turning point.

## Decisions

Some events demand an explicit choice. A decision is not resolved immediately as a separate minigame; the chosen option becomes a directive for the next jump so the wider simulation can absorb it.

## Scheduled Events

Scheduled events are known pressures on the timeline. They give the player something to prepare for and help the simulation avoid feeling arbitrary.

## Reading Briefings

Briefings should be read as strategic intelligence. Look for:

- What changed since the last jump.
- Which actors gained or lost leverage.
- Which promises became liabilities.
- Which new risks need a directive.
